# MarinaFrame: product positioning (2026-11-11)

Status: working position from the PM/CEO discussion of 2026-11-11. Fleet figures below are from
memory and are not checked against current sources.

## One line

MarinaFrame turns the drawings an owner already has into a 3D ship model in days, ready for the
repair, conversion or stability job that needs it.

## Why a built ship needs a 3D model

The need comes from events, not from routine use:

1. Repairs and steel renewal: cut plates, steel weight, work plan.
2. Conversions and retrofits (CII/EEXI, scrubbers, ballast water, new fuels, energy-saving devices):
   fit, strength, weight, class approval.
3. Stability after changes: lightship, tanks, loading computer.
4. Performance studies (CFD for propeller, bulb, coatings).
5. Surveys and class: thickness readings on plates in 3D.
6. Damage and emergencies: damaged stability, fast.
7. Digital twin and fuel optimization.

Most owners have only 2D drawings (PDF or paper). Designers and yards design in 3D, but the 3D model
usually stays with them, is lost, or does not exist for older ships.

## Who it is for (in order)

1. Conversion and repair designers, and repair yards (they work against a dock date).
2. Fleet technical managers (dry-dock planning, retrofits, steel renewal).
3. Class societies and surveyors (later: 3D hull condition records).

## What we promise

- Speed: a usable model in days, not weeks.
- Honesty: each surface and part shows where it came from: read from the drawing, or a labelled guess.
  The output is a starting model for engineers. Never "approved" or "verified".
- Proof: published accuracy on held-out real ships (next: Cama Beach Skiff No. 22, Davis Boat).
- Ready for the next step: STEP, hydrostatics, OCX with plate IDs.

## Way in, then the lasting product

- Way in: drawings to 3D for ships in service. Ships live 25-30+ years and new ships are a few percent
  of the fleet each year, so drawing-only ships stay the majority for 15-20+ years.
- Lasting product: keep the model correct over the ship's life: gauging on plate IDs, wastage against
  class rules, steel renewal lists, repair work packages.
- Accept OCX as an input, not only as an output, so new ships delivered with a yard model use the same
  workflow. (Backlog: step 2.)

## What we do not say

- Not "AI ship designer"; not "replaces naval architects". We save them the remodelling.
- Not "approved" or "verified". Class and engineers approve.
- Not "any drawing, perfect result". We state measured accuracy and show what we guessed.

## Against Forge

| | Forge | MarinaFrame |
|---|---|---|
| Delivery | Hosted, governed pilots | Desktop app, data stays with the customer |
| Gaps in drawings | Refuses to fill | Fills with labelled guesses |
| Accuracy evidence | None published | Published results on held-out real ships |

## Sales channels

Event-driven demand: sell to the people who handle the events (repair yards, conversion designers,
class, fleet technical managers before dry-docking). Turnaround matters more than price.
